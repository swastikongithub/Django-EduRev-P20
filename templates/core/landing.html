01The problem

One room. Four registers. Nobody holds the whole day.

On most campuses a room's day is split across a timetable spreadsheet, a department register, a maintenance log and a trail of emails. Each one is right on its own. Together they collide, and the people at the door find out first.

{# ── 05 The guarantee ─────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

05The guarantee

Two requests. One hour. One booking.

When two people book the same slot in the same instant, the decision isn't left to application code. PostgreSQL itself refuses the second overlapping claim, and that person is told straight away to pick another time.

{# ── 06 Operations ───────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

06Operations

The other side of the desk.

The people who run the rooms work from the same ledger: what students see and what the facility office decides never drift apart.

  • {% icon "list-checks" 22 %}

    Approval workflows

    Chains per resource type and requester role. A student's equipment request goes to its custodian; the Central Auditorium needs its custodian, then the facility manager.

    Custodians · Heads · Facility office
  • {% icon "calendar-days" 22 %}

    Timetable publishing

    Import a term's timetable and publish it. Every class takes its room for the term; bookings in the way are cancelled with a reason, and their owners are told what else is free.

    Facility office
  • {% icon "wrench" 22 %}

    Maintenance and breakdowns

    Schedule a window and the resource drops out of availability. Anyone who finds something broken can report it from the resource's page.

    Custodians · Facility office
  • {% icon "qr-code" 22 %}

    QR check-in at the door

    Every door gets a printable QR code. Scanning it checks the booking in on the spot, inside the check-in window set for that type of resource.

    Everyone · Custodians
  • {% icon "rows-3" 22 %}

    Live booking board

    Every resource's day on one screen, read straight from the ledger, so the people who run the rooms see exactly what students see.

    Custodians · Heads · Facility office
  • {% icon "sliders-horizontal" 22 %}

    Rules, stock and the audit log

    Opening hours, blackouts, quotas and lead times per type; consumables with restock and write-off; and a searchable, exportable record of administrative actions.

    Facility office · Admins

{# ── 08 Engineering ──────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

08Engineering

Built like infrastructure, because a timetable depends on it.

Django 5.2 and PostgreSQL, with Celery and Redis for work done outside a request. The interesting parts are the ones you never see.

  • Correctness

    Rules in the database

    Range types and exclusion constraints make overlaps impossible to store, not merely unlikely.

  • Transactions

    All or nothing

    A booking and its ledger entry are written in one transaction. If either is refused, neither exists.

  • Access

    Checked on the server

    Every page and API call checks role and scope. Administrators and the facility office sign in with two factors.

  • Accountability

    An audit trail

    Administrative actions are recorded with who, what and when, and can be exported.

  • Integration

    A versioned API

    A REST API under /api/v1, described by an OpenAPI schema.

  • Accessibility

    Built for everyone

    Keyboard-first, readable without colour, checked with axe in light and dark themes, in English, Hindi and Punjabi.

  • Verification

    Tested on every push

    Seven CI gates: lint, infrastructure, security scans, Django tests, browser and accessibility journeys, a Docker smoke test and an OWASP ZAP scan.

  • Operations

    Health and recovery

    Liveness and readiness checks, containerised deploys, and documented backup and restore.
